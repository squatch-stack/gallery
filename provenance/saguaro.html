An interior is panned, not orbited, and every card now turns at one speed
 
The springhouse card was a brown blur because a turntable of an interior
orbits outside its own walls. The viewer places the camera at span x d
from the centre and looks inward, so a small distance puts it in the
room: at d=0.35 the sweep shows the mossy barrel vault and the doorway
with the light coming through, and at d=0.60 the camera is already
outside the stonework. Level, not tilted, because a room is read from
standing height.
 
The split-rail fence card was black for a duller reason -- it was
captured mid-load -- and now shows rails against sky.
 
**The cards were spinning at three different speeds and I nearly shipped
it.** minterpolate is not deterministic: fed sixteen frames it returned
clips of 2.27, 2.53 and 3.53 seconds depending on how well it could
estimate motion on that particular scene. Pinning the output with a
trailing fps filter and -r makes the length exact, and all thirteen are
now 3.200 s.
 
So the encode recipe moved out of a shell history and into the tool.
`splat_turntable.py --encode DIR` writes the mp4 and the poster and then
*verifies the duration it asked for*, failing loudly rather than
accepting whatever ffmpeg produced. That check is the part worth having:
nothing else in the pipeline would have noticed, and I only caught it by
comparing a new card against the old ones.
 
616 tests, a11y 24/24.
